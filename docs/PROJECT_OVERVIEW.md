# TrustScore Project Overview

## Purpose

TrustScore is a proposed financial inclusion platform that gives participating financial institutions an additional view of an applicant's financial behaviour beyond conventional credit history. It is designed for self-employed people, informal workers, small-business owners, people with irregular income, and people who have not previously borrowed.

TrustScore is not a replacement for a lender's underwriting, affordability, compliance, or responsible-lending obligations. It is not a loan provider and must never turn a TrustScore assessment into an automatic approval or rejection.

## The inclusion problem

Conventional records can be useful but incomplete. A person may have responsible, verifiable financial behaviour without a traditional credit file. Another person may have little or no independently verifiable evidence. These cases must not be collapsed into one negative outcome.

## Two-pathway model

### Pathway 1: TrustScore-based assessment

Applicants with sufficient, relevant, reliable, and legally accessible evidence may receive an illustrative AI-generated TrustScore. Possible indicators include verified income regularity, permitted utility or rental payment consistency, repayment history, and recognised financial activity. The prototype uses synthetic data and simulated providers.

### Pathway 2: Alternative assessment and assisted access

Applicants with limited evidence receive an alternative verification or assisted assessment where appropriate. If neither conventional nor alternative evidence is sufficient, the system explains the information gap, does not manufacture a score, and may offer a referral or support route. Missing data must never be treated as negative financial behaviour.

## TrustScore

TrustScore is a 0–1,000 supplementary assessment. The proposed 750 benchmark is provisional and exists only as a demonstration reference until calibration and validation are possible. A score is not a person's worth, a repayment guarantee, an affordability result, or a lending decision.

## Operating principles

1. Obtain explicit, informed consent before any permitted data access.
2. Verify identity through an authorised service where one exists; the prototype only simulates this.
3. Collect the minimum relevant data and separate identity from assessment features where feasible.
4. Select the pathway based on evidence sufficiency and quality.
5. Explain the result and its limitations.
6. Preserve human and institutional responsibility for decisions.
7. Adapt permitted features and models to each country; do not assume one model transfers safely.
8. Refer applicants to non-debt support when additional borrowing may be unsuitable.

## Users and value

- Applicants receive a transparent journey and a fair response to missing evidence.
- Financial institutions receive supplementary, explainable information for human review.
- Programme administrators can demonstrate transparent interest assistance funded through a separate allocation.
- Future institutional customers may include banks, microfinance institutions, and other authorised organisations.

## Scope boundaries

The student prototype will use fictional applicants, synthetic financial indicators, a simulated National ID verification flow, and simulated data access. It will not claim partnerships, regulatory approval, real-world accuracy, or production readiness. Country configuration demonstrates extensibility; it does not certify UAE, India, or any other jurisdiction.

## Impact

The primary intended alignment is SDG 1 (No Poverty), with related connections to SDG 8, SDG 9, and SDG 10. Success is not measured only by scores or loan volume. It includes fair treatment of missing evidence, useful supplementary assessment, responsible lending, appropriate support referrals, and measurable safeguards.
